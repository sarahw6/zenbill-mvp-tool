🔎 Patterns Found in Actual Patient Bills
Duplicate Charges (NYU Langone)
EKG/CPT 93010 billed by 3 separate providers on same dates. Classic soft-duplicate: each provider bills independently for reading the same EKG strip. Recoverable: ~$88–$132.
Unit Count Inflation (NYU Langone)
Cardiac Cath Lab billed 177 units × $551 = $97,000 for a single inpatient stay. Unit counts this high require line-by-line itemized audit against medical records. High dispute potential.
CT Scan Overcharge (Bozeman Health)
CT Scan billed at $7,902 — Medicare rate for CPT 74177 is ~$215. This is 37× Medicare. Insurance reversed payment in 2026, leaving patient $7,190 balance. Price anchor dispute + PPDR.
Respiratory Unbundling (Jackson Purchase)
Hand Held Neb ×4 + Chest Physio ×4 billed separately same day. NCCI edits may require bundling respiratory services. Triple metabolic panels (ABS + Comp + Basic) same day also suspicious. Request HCPCS codes.
Financial Assistance Not Offered (Erlanger)
Bill sent to collections with 10-day deadline — yet Erlanger's OWN financial assistance policy is printed on the same bill. Provider failed to proactively offer assistance. Apply immediately.
Insurance Payment Reversal (Bozeman Health)
United Healthcare processed payment in Sept 2025, then reversed and re-billed $8,807 to patient in Jan 2026. Reversal disputes require EOB audit and formal appeal. File appeal within 180 days.
Large Adjustments Applied (VCU Medical)
$5,427 bill reduced to $150 through $5,277 in contractual adjustments. Example of insurer negotiated rate working correctly — patient only owes copay. Verify adjustment math.
Physician Billed Separately (Froedtert)
Hospital bill states "physician charges will be billed separately by Medical College of Wisconsin." Patients often miss the second bill. Always ask: who else is billing?
Vitamin D Likely Deniable (BCBS-TN)
CPT 82306 Vitamin D ($106) frequently denied without ICD-10 E55.9 or Z13.88. Annotated training bill shows exact structure of BCBS payment/adjustment pattern. Check denial code first.

🔍 Good Faith Estimate — Fair Price Lookup
Uses 2026 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule (CMS Oct. release) + geographic adjustment factors. Medicare rates are the federal floor — hospitals typically charge 2.5–5× Medicare for self-pay patients. Use this to benchmark any charge on your bill.
📊 How to Read These Numbers
Medicare Rate (Floor)
What Medicare pays the provider. Adjusted by your state's Geographic Practice Cost Index (GPCI). This is the federal benchmark — providers cannot charge Medicare patients above this rate.
Hospital Charge Range
Hospitals typically charge 2.5–5× Medicare for uninsured/self-pay patients. The "chargemaster" (list price) is the starting point — always negotiate or dispute from Medicare rates up.
Self-Pay Discount Target
Hospitals with 501(c)(3) status must offer financial assistance. For self-pay patients, target 50–70% off the chargemaster. Many hospitals will accept 1.5–2× Medicare with a prompt-pay discount.
PPDR Trigger
If your final bill exceeds your Good Faith Estimate by more than $400, you can file a Patient-Provider Dispute Resolution (PPDR) through HHS within 120 days. Use the Medicare rate to anchor your counter-offer.
Source: CMS 2026 National Physician Fee Schedule (Oct. release). Conversion factor: $33.29. Geographic adjustments use 2026 GPCI by state. Hospital charge multiples are based on CMS and HCCI research. This is a price benchmark tool, not a binding estimate.
🤖 Building the AI + OCR System: Roadmap
This is an internal build guide for the ZenBill/Atrilium engineering team — what components, data sources, and tools are needed to automate the bill analysis pipeline.
📸 Step 1 — OCR (Bill Scanning)
What to extract from a bill scan:
  • Provider name, NPI, address
  • Patient name, DOB, account number
  • Date(s) of service
  • CPT / HCPCS codes + units + charges
  • Revenue codes (facility bills)
  • Diagnosis codes (ICD-10)
  • Insurer name, claim number, EOB payment
  • Adjustments, discounts, patient balance
  • Good Faith Estimate amount (if present)
Recommended OCR tools:
AWS Textract
Best for structured medical bills. Has "Analyze Document" API for tables + forms. ~$1.50/1,000 pages for form extraction.
Azure Form Recognizer
Pre-built invoice model. Good for EOBs. ~$1.50/1,000 pages.
Google Cloud Vision / Document AI
Strong general OCR. Document AI has healthcare processor. ~$1.50/1,000 pages.
Tesseract.js (browser, free)
Open source, runs in browser. Good enough for clean PDFs. Free but slower.
🧠 Step 2 — AI Analysis Engine
What the AI does with extracted data:
  • CPT Validation: Does the CPT code match the described procedure in the medical record?
  • Price Benchmarking: Compare each line item vs. Medicare rate + GPCI. Flag any charge >3× Medicare as high.
  • Unbundling Detection: Check CMS NCCI (National Correct Coding Initiative) edit tables for codes that should be bundled.
  • Duplicate Detection: Same CPT code, same date, same provider = likely duplicate.
  • GFE Comparison: If GFE on file, compare line-by-line. Flag any new codes not in GFE. Calculate total overage.
  • No Surprises Act Check: Was service emergency? Was provider out-of-network? Automatic NSA flagging.
  • Charity Care Eligibility: Based on income info, calculate FPL% and recommend financial assistance programs.
  • Dispute Path Scoring: Score each detected issue by savings potential and dispute likelihood.
Recommended AI components:
Claude API (Anthropic)
Best for unstructured analysis, letter generation, and complex reasoning about billing rules. ~$0.003 per bill with claude-haiku-4-5.
GPT-4o / OpenAI
Good for structured extraction with JSON mode. ~$0.005/bill for extraction tasks.
CMS NCCI Edits DB
Free CMS database of code pairs that cannot be billed together. Essential for unbundling detection. Download quarterly from CMS.gov.
Medicare PFS RVU File (this tool)
Already embedded: 1,653 CPT codes with 2026 Medicare rates. Update quarterly from CMS.gov.

🔐 HIPAA Compliance Requirements
Required for handling PHI (Protected Health Information):
  • BAA (Business Associate Agreement) with all vendors: AWS, Google, OpenAI/Anthropic, Twilio, etc.
  • Encryption at rest (AES-256) and in transit (TLS 1.2+)
  • Access controls: role-based, audit logs, MFA
  • Minimum necessary standard: only collect what's needed
  • Breach notification policy (60 days)
  • HIPAA Security Officer designation
  • Annual risk assessments
  • Employee training (annually)
  • No training AI on patient PHI without explicit consent + de-identification
Recommended HIPAA-compliant infrastructure: AWS GovCloud, Google Healthcare API (PHI-ready), Azure HIPAA blueprint.

It is the "back-end brain" of ZenBill — receives a bill, identifies which dispute format(s) apply, validates you have the required data, generates the submission package, and tracks outcomes. Think of it like a healthcare EDI clearinghouse, but for disputes instead of claims.
